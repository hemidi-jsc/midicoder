# Hospital Information System - Universal-Fully Brief

## 1. Product Context

### Product Name

HealthCore HIS - Hospital Information System

### Product Type

Comprehensive Hospital Information and Management System for modern healthcare facilities

### Target Market

- **Primary Markets:** North America, Europe, Asia-Pacific
- **Customer Segments:** Hospitals with 50-1,000+ beds
- **Facility Types:** General acute-care hospitals, specialty hospitals (cardiac, oncology, orthopedic), community medical centers, teaching hospitals, trauma centers
- **Geographic Focus:** Multi-national deployment with localization for regulatory requirements
- **Organization Sizes:** 100-10,000+ clinical and administrative staff

### Problem Statement

Hospitals face critical operational and clinical challenges in modern healthcare environments:

1. **Patient Care Coordination:** Managing complex, multi-disciplinary care across departments, specialists, shifts, and care settings. Fragmented workflows lead to communication gaps, duplicated tests, medication errors, and delayed treatments. Care transitions between departments (ED to inpatient, inpatient to surgery, inpatient to discharge) are particularly vulnerable to errors.

2. **Electronic Health Records Complexity:** Maintaining comprehensive, accurate, and accessible patient data across the care continuum. Interoperability challenges with external systems (clinics, labs, pharmacies, other hospitals) create information silos. Documentation burden contributes to provider burnout.

3. **Clinical Workflow Efficiency:** Delivering safe, timely patient care while managing increasing regulatory requirements, quality metrics, and patient volume. Manual processes and disconnected systems waste clinician time that should be spent on patient care.

4. **Resource Management Optimization:** Efficiently allocating beds, staff, equipment, and operating rooms to maximize utilization while maintaining quality and safety. Bed turnover delays, OR underutilization, and staff scheduling challenges directly impact revenue and patient access.

5. **Billing and Revenue Cycle Complexity:** Managing complex insurance landscapes with hundreds of payers, varying coverage rules, prior authorization requirements, and coding requirements. Delayed or denied claims significantly impact cash flow. Accurate coding requires clinical documentation improvement.

6. **Compliance and Regulatory Burden:** Meeting numerous regulatory requirements including HIPAA privacy/security, Joint Commission accreditation, Medicare/Medicaid conditions of participation, state-specific regulations, Meaningful Use/ Promoting Interoperability, and quality reporting programs.

7. **Patient Engagement and Communication:** Providing patients access to their health information, enabling secure communication with care teams, scheduling appointments, and understanding bills. Patient expectations for digital access continue to increase.

8. **Data Analytics and Quality Improvement:** Leveraging clinical and operational data for quality improvement, population health management, value-based care programs, and strategic decision-making. Data must be accurate, timely, and accessible.

### Solution Overview

HealthCore HIS provides a comprehensive, integrated platform for modern hospital operations:

- **Patient Administration:** Registration, demographics, insurance verification, consent management, bed management, ADT (Admission/Discharge/Transfer)
- **Clinical Documentation:** Electronic Health Records (EHR) with specialty-specific templates, progress notes, assessments, care plans, problem lists, medication lists
- **Computerized Provider Order Entry (CPOE):** Orders for medications, laboratories, radiology, procedures, consultations, diets, therapies
- **Results Management:** Laboratory, radiology, pathology, and other diagnostic results with critical value alerting
- **Medication Management:** e-Prescribing, medication reconciliation, barcode medication administration (BCMA), automated dispensing cabinet integration
- **Revenue Cycle Management:** Charge capture, medical coding support, claims generation, denial management, payment posting
- **Scheduling:** Appointments, procedures, operating rooms, resources, staff
- **Nursing Applications:** Nursing assessments, flowsheets, care plans, handoff communication, vital signs documentation
- **Specialty Applications:** Emergency Department, Operating Room, Labor & Delivery, Oncology, Cardiology, Critical Care
- **Patient Portal:** Health records access, appointment scheduling, secure messaging, bill pay, health education
- **Clinical Decision Support:** Drug-drug interactions, drug-allergy alerts, clinical guidelines, order sets, quality measure support
- **Reporting and Analytics:** Clinical, operational, financial, and quality reports with dashboard visualization

### Business Model

- **Licensing Model:** Per-bed, per-month subscription with tiered pricing based on hospital size and module selection
- **Implementation Services:** Professional services for configuration, data migration, training, and go-live support
- **Ongoing Support:** 24/7 help desk, system updates, regulatory compliance updates
- **Marketplace:** Third-party applications and integrations through partner ecosystem
- **Success-Based Pricing:** Optional component tied to quality measure achievement or revenue cycle performance

### Competitive Landscape

- **Direct Competitors:** Epic, Cerner (Oracle), Meditech, Allscripts, NextGen
- **Differentiators:** Modern user interface, faster implementation, lower total cost of ownership, superior interoperability, better mobile experience
- **Market Position:** Challenger targeting community hospitals and health systems seeking to upgrade from legacy systems or replace aging technology

### KPI Calculation Methodology

**30-Day Readmission Rate:** Number of unplanned readmissions within 30 days of discharge / Total discharges × 100, risk-adjusted using CMS methodology for target conditions.

**Bed Occupancy Rate:** (Total patient bed days / (Number of beds × Days in period)) × 100, calculated daily and by unit.

**Days in A/R:** (Total A/R balance / Total net charges) × Days in period, calculated weekly for cash flow management.

**Claim Denial Rate:** (Number of denied claims / Total claims submitted) × 100, tracked by payer and denial reason.

**Medication Error Rate:** (Number of medication errors / Total medication doses administered) × 1,000, tracked through voluntary reporting and BCMA alerts.

**Hospital-Acquired Infection Rate:** (Number of HAIs / Total patient days) × 1,000, tracked per NHSN definitions by infection type.

## 4. Core User Journeys (10+)

### J01: Patient Registration and Admission

**Actors:** Registration Specialist, Patient/Guardian, System, Bed Management

**Pre-conditions:**

- Patient presents for admission (scheduled or unscheduled)
- Registration station with access to HIS
- Insurance information available from patient
- Bed availability in appropriate unit

**Flow:**

1. Registration specialist greets patient and identifies admission type (emergency, urgent, scheduled)
2. Specialist searches for existing patient record using name, DOB, SSN
3. If patient exists:
    - Retrieve existing demographic information
    - Update any changed information (address, phone, insurance)
    - Verify current insurance coverage
4. If patient is new:
    - Collect demographic information (name, DOB, address, phone, SSN, gender, race, ethnicity)
    - Collect insurance information (primary, secondary, tertiary)
    - Create new patient record with unique MRN
    - Check for potential duplicates
5. Verify insurance eligibility electronically:
    - Check active coverage status
    - Verify benefits for inpatient services
    - Check for authorization requirements
    - Capture copay, deductible, and coinsurance information
6. Determine financial responsibility:
    - Identify self-pay or financial assistance eligibility
    - Collect copay if required
    - Set up payment arrangement if needed
7. Obtain necessary consents and forms:
    - General consent for treatment
    - HIPAA acknowledgments
    - Financial responsibility agreement
    - Specific procedure consents if applicable
8. Capture clinical information:
    - Allergies and adverse reactions
    - Code status (full code, DNR, etc.)
    - Advance directives if available
    - Emergency contact information
9. System assigns bed based on:
    - Acuity and unit requirements
    - Bed availability
    - Patient preferences when possible
    - Isolation requirements if needed
10. Generate admission documentation:
    - Wristband with barcode/RFID
    - Admission paperwork
    - Insurance cards scanned
11. Notify receiving unit of admission:
    - Patient information transmitted
    - Bed assignment communicated
    - Special requirements flagged

**Post-conditions:**

- Patient has active registration with MRN
- Insurance verified and captured
- Consents and forms on file
- Bed assigned and unit notified
- Patient ready for clinical care

**Success Criteria:**

- Registration completed in <15 minutes for scheduled admissions
- 100% insurance verification before admission when possible
- Zero duplicate patient records created
- All required consents obtained
- Bed assignment communication in <5 minutes

**Failure Scenarios:**

- Duplicate patient found: Merge records or link encounters, alert supervisor
- Insurance verification fails: Continue registration, flag for billing follow-up
- No bed available: Place in ED holding or alternate facility, notify admissions
- System downtime: Use paper registration, enter when system restored

### J02: Physician Order Entry (CPOE)

**Actors:** Physician, System, Pharmacist (for medication orders), Nursing Staff

**Pre-conditions:**

- Physician authenticated in HIS
- Patient chart accessible
- Order entry interface available
- Patient has active encounter

**Flow:**

1. Physician accesses patient chart from patient list or schedule
2. Physician reviews current patient status:
    - Active problems
    - Current medications
    - Recent lab results
    - Allergies
3. Physician selects order entry from chart menu
4. Physician chooses order category (medication, lab, radiology, procedure, etc.)
5. For medication orders:
    - Search and select medication from formulary
    - Specify dose, route, frequency, duration
    - System checks for drug-allergy interactions
    - System checks for drug-drug interactions
    - System checks for dose appropriatenity (age, weight, renal function)
    - System alerts if interactions detected
    - Physician acknowledges and overrides if clinically appropriate
    - System suggests standardized order if order set available
6. For laboratory orders:
    - Select tests from categorized test menu
    - System bundles tests if order set defined
    - Specify timing (stat, routine, timed)
    - Add clinical indication if required
7. For radiology orders:
    - Select study type from menu
    - Specify body part and laterality
    - Add clinical indication
    - Check for authorization requirements
8. For procedure orders:
    - Select procedure from menu
    - Specify date, location, laterality
    - Link to ordering physician
    - Check for authorization requirements
9. Physician reviews all new orders in order preview
10. Physician electronically signs order batch
11. System routes orders to appropriate departments:
    - Medications to pharmacy
    - Labs to laboratory
    - Radiology to radiology scheduling
12. System notifies receiving departments of new orders
13. Orders appear in patient chart as active

**Post-conditions:**

- Orders entered and signed in system
- Orders transmitted to executing departments
- Orders documented in patient record
- Appropriate alerts generated

**Success Criteria:**

- 95%+ of orders entered through CPOE
- Order entry time <2 minutes per order
- 100% of orders include clinical decision support checks
- Zero orders transmitted with critical errors
- Order acknowledgment within 5 minutes

**Failure Scenarios:**

- CDS alert overrides tracked and reviewed for appropriateness
- Order transmission fails: Retry automatically, alert if persistent
- System downtime: Verbal orders with documentation, enter when restored
- Formulary restriction: Alert physician, require override justification

### J03: Nursing Assessment and Documentation

**Actors:** Nurse, System, Physician

**Pre-conditions:**

- Nurse authenticated in HIS
- Patient assigned to nurse's care
- Nursing workstation available
- Admission or shift change assessment due

**Flow:**

1. Nurse accesses patient chart from assignment list
2. Nurse selects nursing documentation module
3. System presents appropriate assessment based on context:
    - Admission assessment for new admissions
    - Shift assessment for routine documentation
    - Post-procedure assessment after procedures
4. Nurse completes assessment sections:
    - Vital signs (BP, HR, RR, temp, O2 sat, pain)
    - Physical assessment (head-to-toe or focused)
    - Functional status (ambulation, ADLs)
    - Skin assessment (integrity, pressure risk)
    - Pain assessment (location, intensity, characteristics)
    - Neurological status (LOC, orientation)
    - Intake and output
5. System performs automated calculations:
    - Fall risk score (Morse or HAC)
    - Pressure ulcer risk (Braden)
    - Pain severity categorization
6. System generates alerts for abnormal findings:
    - Vital sign outliers trigger notification
    - High fall risk triggers precautions
    - High pressure risk triggers interventions
7. Nurse reviews and addresses alerts:
    - Implements appropriate precautions
    - Notifies physician if required
    - Documents interventions
8. Nurse reviews and updates care plan:
    - Nursing diagnoses
    - Goals and outcomes
    - Interventions
9. Nurse documents interventions performed:
    - Medications administered
    - Treatments completed
    - Patient education provided
    - Precautions implemented
10. Nurse saves and signs assessment
11. System updates patient status and alerts care team:
    - Critical findings flagged
    - Physician notification if required
    - Care plan accessible to team

**Post-conditions:**

- Assessment documented in patient record
- Risk scores calculated and displayed
- Alerts generated for abnormal findings
- Care plan updated
- Care team informed of status

**Success Criteria:**

- Admission assessment completed within 4 hours of admission
- Shift assessments completed per unit protocol
- 100% of abnormal findings addressed
- Care plan updated within 24 hours of admission
- Documentation accessible to care team in real-time

**Failure Scenarios:**

- Incomplete assessment: System prevents checkout until complete
- Critical value: System forces notification acknowledgment
- System downtime: Paper documentation, enter when restored
- Assessment due alert: Escalate to charge nurse if overdue

### J04: Medication Administration (BCMA)

**Actors:** Nurse, System, Pharmacy

**Pre-conditions:**

- Medication order active and verified by pharmacy
- Medication available in ADC or pharmacy
- BCMA scanner available
- Patient has scannable wristband

**Flow:**

1. Nurse accesses patient's MAR (Medication Administration Record)
2. System displays due medications based on schedule
3. Nurse retrieves medication from ADC:
    - Authenticate at ADC
    - Scan patient barcode
    - Select and remove medication
    - System tracks removal to patient
4. Nurse brings medication and scanner to patient room
5. Nurse performs "five rights" verification:
    - Scan patient wristband (right patient)
    - Scan medication barcode (right medication)
    - System verifies right dose, right route, right time
6. System validates administration:
    - Checks order is active
    - Checks no allergies to medication
    - Checks no contraindications
    - Checks dose appropriateness
    - Checks timing within allowed window

## 7. Domain Rules and Invariants (10+)

### INV01: Patient Cannot Have Duplicate MRN

**Rule:** Each patient must have a unique Medical Record Number (MRN) within the health system.

**Rationale:** Duplicate MRNs would cause patient safety risks through misidentification, fragmented records, and treatment errors.

**Enforcement:** System validates uniqueness before MRN assignment; duplicate detection algorithms run on registration.

**Exception:** None - MRN uniqueness is absolute.

---

### INV02: Allergy Alert Before Medication Administration

**Rule:** System must alert when a medication order or administration conflicts with documented patient allergies.

**Rationale:** Allergic reactions can be life-threatening; early detection prevents harm.

**Enforcement:** Real-time allergy checking on order entry and administration; hard stop for severe allergies.

**Exception:** Allergist may document intentional exposure for desensitization with documentation.

---

### INV03: Drug Interaction Warning on Order Entry

**Rule:** System must check for drug-drug interactions when medication orders are entered.

**Rationale:** Drug interactions can cause adverse effects, reduced efficacy, or toxicity.

**Enforcement:** CDS engine checks all active medications against new orders; alerts displayed before sign.

**Exception:** Clinician may override with documented rationale after clinical judgment.

---

### INV04: Provider Credentials Must Be Valid

**Rule:** Only providers with active, validated credentials can enter orders or sign documentation.

**Rationale:** Ensures only qualified personnel provide care; legal and regulatory requirement.

**Enforcement:** System checks credential status before allowing order entry; automatic deactivation on expiration.

**Exception:** Temporary privileges for visiting physicians with documented approval.

---

### INV05: Bed Cannot Be Double-Booked

**Rule:** A bed can only be assigned to one patient at a time.

**Rationale:** Prevents assignment errors and ensures accurate census and resource allocation.

**Enforcement:** System validates bed availability before assignment; real-time bed status tracking.

**Exception:** Temporary dual use for mother-infant with special bed type designation.

---

### INV06: Lab Result Amendment Requires Audit Trail

**Rule:** Any modification to a released laboratory result must be documented with reason and audit trail.

**Rationale:** Result changes could impact patient care; audit trail supports quality and safety investigations.

**Enforcement:** System requires reason for amendment; original value preserved; change logged with user and timestamp.

**Exception:** None - all amendments require documentation.

---

### INV07: Discharge Requires Required Documentation

**Rule:** Discharge cannot be completed without required documentation elements.

**Rationale:** Incomplete discharge documentation risks patient safety and regulatory compliance.

**Enforcement:** System validates required elements (discharge diagnosis, medications, instructions) before discharge completion.

**Exception:** Emergency discharge with documented justification and follow-up documentation plan.

---

### INV08: Surgery Requires Pre-Operative Clearance

**Rule:** Elective surgery cannot be scheduled without documented pre-operative medical clearance.

**Rationale:** Ensures patient is medically fit for surgery; reduces risk of intraoperative complications.

**Enforcement:** System validates clearance status before allowing OR scheduling; clearance expiration tracking.

**Exception:** Emergency/urgent surgery with documented clinical justification.

---

### INV09: Claim Cannot Submit Without Required Codes

**Rule:** Claims cannot be submitted without required diagnosis and procedure codes.

**Rationale:** Incomplete claims result in denials and revenue loss; regulatory requirement.

**Enforcement:** System validates claim completeness before submission; required codes per payer rules.

**Exception:** None - claims must meet minimum requirements for submission.

---

### INV10: Orders Cannot Be Signed By Unlicensed Users

**Rule:** Only licensed providers with appropriate privileges can sign medical orders.

**Rationale:** Legal requirement that orders come from authorized providers; patient safety.

**Enforcement:** System validates user credentials and privileges before allowing order sign.

**Exception:** Verbal orders entered by licensed personnel with subsequent provider signature within required timeframe.

---

### INV11: Critical Values Require Provider Notification

**Rule:** Critical laboratory and radiology results must be communicated to a provider and acknowledged.

**Rationale:** Delayed notification of critical values can result in patient harm or death.

**Enforcement:** System tracks notification and acknowledgment; escalation if not acknowledged in timeframe.

**Exception:** Patient deceased or discharged before result available with documentation.

---

### INV12: Barcode Verification Required for Medication Administration

**Rule:** Medication administration must include patient and medication barcode scanning.

**Rationale:** Barcode scanning significantly reduces medication administration errors.

**Enforcement:** System requires successful scans before documenting administration; exception workflow with justification.

**Exception:** Wristband damaged or medication unlabeled with documented override and double-check.

---

### INT03A: Prescription Drug Monitoring Program (PDMP)

**Purpose:** Check controlled substance prescribing history before prescribing.

**Details:**

- Real-time PDMP query before controlled substance prescription
- Review patient's prescription history for red flags
- Document PDMP check in patient record
- Support multi-state queries for patients in border regions
- Prescription verification for controlled substances
- Reporting of suspicious prescribing patterns
- Integration with controlled substance prescription workflow

**Red Flag Indicators:**

- Multiple prescribers for same medication class
- Early refill requests
- Patterns suggesting diversion
- Dose/quantity anomalies
- Concurrent prescribing of interacting substances
- Doctor shopping behavior

**Technical Requirements:**

- API integration with state PDMP databases
- Single-sign-on or secure authentication
- NCPDP SCRIPT 2014 or state-specific API
- Multi-state PDMP access (PDMP Interconnect)
- Real-time response (< 3 seconds)
- Audit trail for all queries

**Data Mapping:**

- Query: Patient demographics, Provider DEA number, Provider license
- Response: Prescription history (medication, dose, quantity, prescriber, pharmacy, date)
- Check result: Red flags, anomalies, alerts

**Workflow Integration:**

- **Before Prescribing:**
    - System prompts for PDMP check when controlled substance selected
    - Provider reviews PDMP report
    - Provider documents decision (proceed/decline) with rationale
    - Report saved to patient record

- **During Prescription:**
    - System validates DEA number and license
    - System checks for state-specific requirements
    - System logs prescription for PDMP reporting

- **After Prescription:**
    - System submits prescription data to PDMP
    - System generates monthly PDMP reports for review

**State Compliance Requirements:**

- **Mandatory States:** PDMP check required before each controlled substance prescription
- **Frequency Requirements:**
    - Schedule II: Check before each prescription
    - Schedule III-V: Check at least every 90 days or before each prescription
- **Documentation:** PDMP check and review documented in patient record
- **Exemptions:** Emergency situations, palliative/hospice care

**Error Handling:**

- PDMP system unavailable: Alert provider, allow override with documentation
- Query timeout: Retry with exponential backoff
- Authentication failure: Re-authenticate workflow
- Multi-state query partial failure: Show available data, alert to missing states

**Implementation:**

- PDMP interface configuration per state
- Controlled substance workflow integration
- Red flag alert system
- Audit logging for compliance
- Provider training on PDMP use

**Evidence:**

- PDMP query logs
- Provider review documentation
- Red flag alert responses
- Multi-state query confirmations
